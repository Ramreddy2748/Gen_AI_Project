## Comparison to baseline (GPT-4o-mini, same prompt and data)

| Metric | GPT-4o-mini | DeepSeek-chat | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Window accuracy | 60.66% | **62.50%** | +1.84 |
| Window fall recall | 48.31% | **50.85%** | +2.54 |
| Window fall F1 | 51.58% | **54.05%** | +2.47 |
| Video accuracy | **66.67%** | 64.91% | −1.76 |
| Video fall recall | 58.33% | **62.50%** | +4.17 |
| Video fall F1 | 59.57% | **60.00%** | +0.43 |

At the video level, GPT-4o-mini caught 14 of 24 falls with 9 false alarms; DeepSeek caught 15 of 24 with 11 false alarms. DeepSeek correctly flagged one additional real fall while raising two additional false positives. For a safety-critical setting where missing a fall is more costly than raising one false alarm, this is a net positive — but the magnitude of the lift is small, and Few-Shot remains a relatively saturated strategy on this dataset.

## Takeaway

Swapping GPT-4o-mini for DeepSeek-chat on the existing Few-Shot prompt gives a modest, consistent improvement across most metrics. The clearest gain is on video-level fall recall (+4.2 percentage points), which is the metric that matters most for elderly-care deployment. Other metrics move by 1–3 points in either direction, well within run-to-run noise for this prompt strategy. This run does not change which strategy is the project's strongest — RAG and Best-Prompt still dominate Few-Shot regardless of the model — but it does suggest DeepSeek is a viable, cheap drop-in replacement for the cheaper-tier OpenAI model.
